philosophy & ethics

    Below are the core principles and key ideas that shape my scholarly work. I was inspired to structure and document my scientific philosophy and ethics by this lovely example from the Green Neuroscience Laboratory.

    Years after I started asking my students to reflect on and share out loud their personal ethics and philosophies of science, I've realised that this is a first step towards scientific accountability -- to name what we value, to declare our boundaries and our redlines, to envision how we can best relate to each other while pursuing curiosity and knowledge. This is an exercise in asking ourselves, what principles are we willing to commit to publicly, and ask our comrades and dear ones to expect from us?

    Core ideas

    the freedoms to doubt, to fail, and to change

    My core practice as a scholar is built on three fundamental freedoms -- to doubt, to fail, and to change.

    Being free to doubt means to support and validate the constant re-examination what we think we know, with honesty, curiosity, and an openness to critical interrogation. Being free to fail means to consider mistakes as moments of vulnerability, clarity, and opportunity. Being free to change means forgiving each other, so that everyone can evolve and we don't trap each other in past versions of ourselves.

    I am committed to building learning spaces that nurture these freedoms and grapple with the vast differences that people can experience when it comes to their ability to access these freedoms and the consequences they face for exercising these freedoms.

    we think with all of our bodies

    Our bodies are living examples of effective and resilient coordination amongst a massive and incredibly diverse collective. This perspective is crucial to my research as a neuroscientist, to my practices as an artist, and to my strategies as a community organizer.

    This perspective feels important to advocate at a time when I observe a hyperfocus in modern neuroscience research on the parts of our nervous system contained inside our heads. To me this flagrantly disregards the enormously interconnected workings of our entire nervous system -- and complex biological systems in general -- and teaches us unhelpful intuitions about our bodies and ecologies. I am committed to cultivating a wholistic understanding of our anatomy and physiology, and to use that knowledge for the advancement of collective liberation.

    the value of joy

    I believe that joy is a necessary condition for learning, a belief well-supported by research in neuroscience and psychology, and by my personal lived experiences. I am committed to honoring my own joy and nurturing the joy of others.

    life-oriented scholarship

    In everything I do as a scholar, I seek to honor life. There is a great deal of harm and suffering that has been justified in the pursuit of knowledge. I'm determined to cultivate the humility and patience to learn from this history and find paths forward that do not coerce, exploit, or extract.

    imagination is a vital muscle

    Our imaginations are our greatest superpower, our most extraordinary muscle, and the ultimate purpose of our nervous systems. It is our privilege and our responsibility to exercise our imaginations, to flex this muscle that we have been evolving and honing ever since we could name ourselves as humans.

    To be truly visionary we have to root our imagination in our concrete reality while simultaneously imagining possibilities beyond that reality. Feminism Is for Everybody: Passionate Politics, by bell hooks, 2014.
